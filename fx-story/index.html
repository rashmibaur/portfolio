Objective

Objective

The goal of this project was to transform a large, complex dataset (originally in an Excel sheet containing BT team data such as projects, revenue, duration, and engagement types) into a clear, engaging story for leaders and employees.

  • Visibility

    Provide visibility into team performance and business development (BD) activities.

  • Revenue

    Help stakeholders understand where revenue is coming from.

  • Work distribution

    Offer insights into work distribution, engagement types, and workforce composition.

  • Storytelling

    Present insights in a visually engaging and easy-to-consume storytelling format.

Data

Data exploration & analysis

Understanding & structuring the data. The dataset consisted of multiple attributes spanning projects, finance, workforce, and utilization metrics. To make analysis meaningful, the data was first grouped into logical buckets.

Business & project context

  • Month-Year
  • Project Name
  • Project Categorization
  • Classification Name
  • Vertical

Financial metrics

  • Revenue
  • Billed Utilization Applicable

Workforce attributes

  • Employee Name
  • Grade
  • Location
  • Function Name

Utilization & capacity

  • Provisional Billed FTE
  • Provisional Unbilled FTE
  • Provisional Overbilled FTE
  • Pre-SoW FTE
  • FTE
  • Overstaffed FTE

Preparation

Data cleaning & preparation

Standardization

  • Ensured consistent naming for project categories (e.g., “Billed”, “Bench”, “Internal”) and for verticals and functions
  • Converted Month-Year into a time format for chronological analysis

Filtering key segments

Created subsets such as:

  • Billed projects → using Billed Utilization Applicable
  • Active workforce → excluding leave/bench where needed

Handling missing data

  • Checked for nulls in revenue and FTE columns
  • Missing values were either imputed (if minor) or excluded from aggregates

Creating core metrics

Workforce metrics

  • Total FTE = SUM(FTE)
  • Employee Count = COUNT(Employee Name)

Capacity indicators

  • Bench Strength = SUM(Overstaffed FTE)
  • Pipeline Work = SUM(Pre-SoW FTE)

Utilization breakdown

  • Billed FTE = SUM(Provisional Billed FTE)
  • Unbilled FTE = SUM(Provisional Unbilled FTE)
  • Overbilled FTE = SUM(Provisional Overbilled FTE)

Revenue metrics

  • Total Revenue = SUM(Revenue)
  • Revenue by Vertical = SUM(Revenue) grouped by Vertical
  • Revenue by Project = SUM(Revenue) grouped by Project Name

Exploration

Exploratory analysis

  • Time-based analysis

    Trend of employee allocation across months: COUNT(Employee Name) by Month-Year + Project Categorization.

  • Workforce distribution

    FTE split across vertical, project and function.

  • Workforce composition

    Distribution by grade, function and location.

  • Revenue contribution

    Revenue contribution across verticals, accounts, functions and grades.

  • Utilization efficiency

    Compared billed vs unbilled vs overbilled FTE. Identified idle capacity and overutilization.

Findings

Patterns & insights

High concentration patterns

Certain verticals and accounts dominate FTE allocation and revenue contribution.

Workforce insights

  • Mid-senior grades contribute most to revenue
  • UI/UX & Design Research heavily engaged in delivery

Utilization trends

  • “Billed” category consistently highest across months
  • Bench and internal categories fluctuate significantly

Operational gaps

Presence of:

  • Unbilled FTE (WIP)
  • Overstaffed FTE (inefficiency)
  • Pre-SoW (future opportunities)

Audience

End users want to know

Business development & projects

  • What projects are currently active?
  • Which verticals or categories are growing?
  • What types of engagements are ongoing?

Financial performance

  • How much revenue is each project generating?
  • Which verticals/accounts drive the most revenue?
  • How does revenue change over time (Month-Year)?

Utilization & work distribution

  • What is the billed vs unbilled utilization?
  • Where do we have WIP (unbilled FTE)?
  • Are there projects with overbilling?

Resource allocation

  • Where are employees allocated (projects, locations)?
  • What is the grade distribution across projects?
  • Which functions are heavily staffed?

Visualisations

Data visualisations

Six charts, each chosen to answer one question. For every chart: the data points used, the calculations behind it, what it shows, what it tells us, and why that chart type.

Chart 01 · Bubble / packed circle

Time spent across accounts

A packed bubble chart was used to represent FTE distribution across verticals and accounts, allowing stakeholders to instantly identify where the team's time is concentrated.

Data points

  • Vertical
  • FTE
  • Project Name (grouped as accounts/clients)
  • Filter: Project Categorization

Calculations

Total FTE per accountSUM(FTE) grouped by Project Name
Total FTE per verticalSUM(FTE) grouped by Vertical
Hierarchy mappingVertical → Account → (Project level if drilled)

What it shows

  • Circle size = FTE (time spent)
  • Large circles = more time investment
  • Nested circles = accounts inside verticals

Key insights

  • Time spent is highly concentrated in a few key accounts (large bubbles dominate)
  • Certain verticals (e.g., CPG, Internal) absorb disproportionately higher FTE
  • Long tail of smaller accounts shows distributed but low engagement

Why this chart

Where is the team spending time?

  • Relative magnitude (big vs small accounts)
  • Hierarchy visually without axes
  • Drill-down interaction

Chart 02 · Radial / sunburst

Revenue by sector

A radial hierarchy visualization was chosen to show how revenue flows from sectors to individual accounts, making hierarchical relationships more intuitive than traditional charts.

Data points

  • Vertical
  • Project Categorization
  • Project Name
  • Revenue

Calculations

Total revenueSUM(Revenue)
Revenue by verticalSUM(Revenue) grouped by Vertical
Revenue by account within verticalSUM(Revenue) grouped by Vertical + Project Name
Hierarchy mappingVertical → Account → Project (optional drill)

What it shows

  • Center = overall dataset
  • First ring = verticals
  • Outer nodes = accounts / projects
  • Arc length / bar size = revenue contribution

Key insights

  • Revenue is driven by a subset of high-value accounts
  • Some accounts with high FTE do not proportionally contribute to revenue
  • Revenue distribution across sectors is uneven, with clear top-performing verticals

Why this chart

Where is revenue coming from across sectors?

  • Represents hierarchy clearly
  • Shows proportion within each vertical
  • Visually engaging for storytelling

Chart 03 · Horizontal range / bar chart

NPS across billed projects

A horizontal bar chart was used to compare NPS across multiple projects, ensuring readability for long project names and quick identification of satisfaction trends.

Data points

  • Project Name
  • Vertical
  • Billed Utilization Applicable (filter for billed projects)
  • NPS Score

Calculations

Filter: only billed projectsBilled Utilization Applicable = Yes
NPS per projectAVG(NPS) grouped by Project Name
SegmentationHigh NPS (≥7)
Low NPS (<7)

What it shows

  • Each row = a project
  • Bar position = NPS score
  • Color = vertical
  • Clustered on higher side (7+) → key insight

Key insights

  • Majority of projects have high NPS (7 and above), indicating strong delivery quality
  • Limited number of projects fall into lower satisfaction bands
  • Delivery performance is consistently strong across verticals

Why this chart

How satisfied are clients across projects?

  • Easy scanning of many projects
  • Horizontal layout fits long project names
  • Allows quick comparison of distribution

Chart 04 · Heatmap

Employees across project categories over time

A heatmap was used to visualize workforce distribution across project categories over time, enabling quick identification of peak utilization periods and shifts in employee allocation.

Data points

  • Month-Year
  • Project Categorization
  • Employee Name or FTE as proxy for people count
  • Filter: Grade

Calculations

Count of employees per category per monthCOUNT(Employee Name)
grouped by Month-Year + Project Categorization
Normalization for color scaleMin = 0
Max ≈ 160
Time sequencingMonth-Year sorted chronologically

What it shows

  • X-axis = time (months)
  • Y-axis = project categories (Billed, Bench, Internal, Leave, etc.)
  • Color intensity = number of employees

Key insights

  • Billed category consistently high (bright yellow band)
  • Bench / blocked / leaves fluctuate
  • Capability & internal work spread more evenly

Why this chart

When and where are people working the most?

  • Shows 2 dimensions simultaneously (time + category)
  • Highlights trends over time, peaks and dips instantly
  • Easy to spot stable vs fluctuating categories and workforce shifts

Narrative

Storyline: from effort to impact

The story begins with key foundational questions, each answered by one chart.

Together, these form a connected narrative loop

  1. Input time

    Where is time being spent?

    FTE distribution across accounts and verticals.

  2. Output revenue

    Where is revenue being generated?

    Revenue contribution across projects and sectors.

  3. Outcome satisfaction

    How well is the work being delivered?

    NPS across billed projects.

  4. Execution when & how work happens

    When are people working the most?

    Employee distribution across project categories over time.

  5. Ownership who does the work

    Who is doing what work?

    Function-level allocation across project categories.

  6. Value drivers who creates impact

    Who is driving value?

    Revenue contribution across functions and grades.

This framing allows stakeholders to

  • Link team effort directly to financial performance
  • Evaluate delivery quality alongside business outcomes
  • Identify when workforce demand peaks and shifts
  • Understand how work is distributed across functions and categories
  • Recognise which roles and seniority levels drive maximum value

Move beyond surface metrics to understand not just what is happening, but how, when, and by whom.
